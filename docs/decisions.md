# Decisions

Why the language is the way it is, a row for each decision, and what the July specs have that isn't built.
`language.md` is the reference for what exists. Add a row here when the language changes.

## The language

Newest first. "Why" is the reason at the time, so these can be revisited.

| Date | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-11-21 | An expression's states can each say `on` the beat that brings them, `expression work { on factor: "z.1 ( a + b )" at equation }`, in place of `by` a number. It starts from the state before, is the written state as that beat ends, and holds through every beat no state names; before the first it's empty. States are written in the order their beats run and a beat brings one state, so a state out of order, a beat named twice, and a name that isn't a beat's are errors at the state's line. `on` takes a beat with a length, the only kind with a name. `by` stays, for a number a reader scrubs, and a block has one form or the other. Nothing new in the IR: the driver is the sum of the named beats' progress | Kris's note of 2026-11-21. With `by beat` a state is a row per beat, so the square's `shorten` beat did its work by position: nothing read its name, and making it a pause moved every later state onto the wrong beat, with the scaled arrow moving a beat before its algebra. Three rows of `"z"` were holds that had to be written and counted. Named, the beat says when and the terms say which symbols move, so a story can be retimed without touching the algebra, and a hold isn't written. The order is checked because the driver only counts beats done: out of order it would show the wrong state with no error. The explainer's roulette already wrote the same sum by hand (`let said = name_hub + …`, `by said`), which is why the IR needed nothing. Not chosen: dropping `by`, since a param a reader drags through successive approximations has no beats |
| 2026-11-21 | A `poster` that says `time` and holds a param that follows time is an error: `a poster says time or holds a, and not both`. Either alone is taken, and so is `time` with a param time doesn't drive. The command has the same rule for its stills and video: `--t` with a `--param` that follows time is an error, a video refuses such a `--param`, and `--t` hands back one the poster holds. | Kris: only a handle and time take turns driving the same param, and outside a page there are no turns, so saying both for one frame doesn't make sense. Before, the held value won without a word and the time changed nothing the param drew. An error is the explicit choice: either reading could be meant, and picking one would hide the other. |
| 2026-11-19 | After the sheets are applied, a mark that would paint nothing gets its type's default paint in the default ink: a 1 px stroke for what's a line and a region's outline, a fill for a dot and a collection of dots. A property a sheet wrote picks the paint it belongs to and is kept (`width` or `dash` alone is a stroke, on a dot too; `radius` or `fill_opacity` alone a fill). A mark that paints anything, a side's fill included, is left as written. Decided once in the core, where styles are resolved, so the oracle, the shader, SVG, video and the element agree | Kris: "too confusing not to have default styles". Tangents with an empty sheet showed its five letters on white and nothing else. Not a built-in base sheet, since every property would inherit from it and the glow grid, styled only with side fills, would get a stroke it never asked for. The census found two corpus marks hidden by being left unstyled, the hearts' ties in the glow look, which now say `stroke: none`, and no other look changes |
| 2026-11-19 | `stroke: none` and `fill: none` mean no paint, and that paint gets no default, each paint on its own: `fill: none` hides a dot, and `stroke: none` on a dot with nothing else still fills it. Every other property's `none` still takes back what an earlier rule set. `show: 0` stays the way to make a mark absent | With defaults, hiding is something a sheet says. SVG and CSS already mean this by `none` for a paint, and the reference already said "`stroke: none` draws no line". Not `draw: none`, since `draw` is a rendering hint and can't change what a figure means, and not `opacity: 0` alone, which a fade passes through. No corpus look changes: wherever `none` took back a paint, the paint stays off |
| 2026-11-19 | The default ink is the sheets' `ink`, if one names a color that, or else black or white, whichever has more contrast with the paper by WCAG's ratio, judged frame by frame for a paper that changes. Labels, terms, a panel's title and formula, and the caption with no `fill` take it too, where they were black | The theme already calls its text and line color `ink`, so a figure under it draws in the theme's color, and with no sheet in black on white. A dark paper with nothing else said would otherwise hide every default mark and every label. The choice is the internal `Contrast` of the paper's fill, which `eval` and the shader both evaluate, so a paper that changes needed nothing more |
| 2026-11-19 | A label's `place` defaults to `settle`, and `place: fixed` says it stays exactly at its offset. A label written on its own point starts a little up and to the right, since a bump pushes nowhere at its center. Unroll's two looks and bounds' blog look say `place: fixed` for the labels they placed by hand | With default paint, `label z at z` beside `dot z` drew the dot over its own label, the first thing a reader of a bare figure sees. Settling moved those three looks' labels by up to 22 px, and Kris, asked: "Can't [that] be solved by overriding it back to fixed?" It can, so the default is the one a figure with nothing said needs, and the looks that placed theirs by hand say so |
| 2026-11-19 | `curves` is a type a selector can name, as `dots` and `circles` are. A sheet's `ink` that isn't a color is an error at its line. `fill: none` on a panel's `title`, its `formula` or the `caption` leaves them undrawn, as on a label; on the `paper`, and without a facet on what's filled only by its sides, it's refused with the reason. A property asks for a default paint only on a mark it applies to, so `radius` on a region asks for nothing | Found in review of the defaults. A `curves` collection was tagged only `curves`, which no rule by type could reach, so a shared sheet couldn't style one and the corpus guard couldn't see one. An `ink` of `3` was silently passed over for black or white, and a typo in a theme's would have been. The reference said `fill: none` was no paint, and the three kinds of text it named as taking the ink refused it |
| 2026-11-19 | A label's mathematics (its `name`, `expr` and `value`) and a term are laid out as mathematics whatever the `font`, which only chooses the face for words. `font` still defaults to `sans`, and no other label property gets a default | Kris's hearts labels came out as raw `z_b = 1.48e^(1.12i)` in sans because the layout of a value hung on the face. Whether `e^(1.12i)` has a superscript isn't a choice of typeface. A halo or settling is a choice, not something whose absence is broken, so they aren't defaults. Words stay words, so the theme's sans tick labels, `"+{q}"`, don't change, and no corpus look does |
| 2026-11-19 | A constant is a number, a constant such as `π`, `duration`, or a `let` or a call to the figure's own `fn` made only of those, written anywhere in the figure. Every place that needs a constant takes one: `duration`, a pause, a beat's length, a param's step, a poster's values, and a viewport's bounds and pad; a range's end may read params as before, and any `let` it reads has to be constant. A constant is a finite number, and `duration` one above 0, checked at its statement. A param isn't one, though it has a default, and neither is `time`, a choice, a beat or anything that reads one; the error names what moves and the `let` it moves | Kris, editing hearts in the playground, wanted to say its 12 once, and `let period = 12` then `duration period` was refused: only literals and `π` counted. A `let` of numbers can't change, so refusing it only made a figure repeat itself. What a reader or the clock moves would freeze at time 0, so it stays an error. Lets are found wherever they're written, as everywhere else. A `duration` of `0/0` reached the GPU as `NaNf` and failed in the shader, so a value that isn't a finite number is refused where it's written |
| 2026-11-19 | An expression can read `duration`, the number the figure's `duration` statement says, in a `let`, a `bind`, a mark and a style, and where a constant is needed. It's an error in the statement itself, in a `let` that statement reads, and in a figure with no statement, even when beats with lengths add up to a duration. It's folded to a number at compile time. A figure with a `duration` statement can't also define `duration` at its top level or say it twice, and the error is at whichever the figure reads later, naming the other's file and line; a parameter called `duration` is local as always, a sheet's own name is the sheet's, and a figure without a statement can take the name for its own value, as with `pi` | Kris wrote `bind theta = mod(time, duration) / duration·2·π` to say hearts' 12 once and got `unknown name`. Without a statement a figure's length comes from its beats, and a length that read it would be circular, so it's refused rather than given a wrong number. As a number, the CPU and the GPU read the same value and nothing downstream knows the name. A statement and a `let` of the same name, or two statements, would make one word mean two things, and the later quietly winning hides which one the figure runs on |
| 2026-11-19 | A pasted `\pm` and `\mp` come through as `±` and `∓`, and a sign before a product brackets it, `±(2x)` | A formula can't say where it goes, and since `±` has two meanings in Pencil, two branches in a collection and an interval as a whole range, refusing it refused the commonest pastes, the quadratic formula and `x = 5 ± 2`. Pencil's own error says why where the text can't take it |
| 2026-11-18 | Where a range is written, `med ± dev` as the whole range is the interval `(med - abs(dev))..(med + abs(dev))`, between its two values whichever is larger, so a negative `dev` never reverses it (decided 2026-11-19 in review): sugar only, so each place keeps its own rules for a range's ends and its `step`. `±x` alone, `∓`, and `±` inside an end are errors; a range over both signs is written with a list, `for s in {1, -1}, k in 0..(2 + s)`. A statement with a `±` can't also name a variable or `with` `sign` | Kris's reading: "med ± dev, sugar for (med - dev)..(med + dev)", the tolerance sense of ±, as x = 5 ± 2. It's the other thing ± means in mathematics, and a range position can only mean that one, since a range is already a set of values. A two-branch range would be two ranges, which nothing reads, so the list says it. `±x` alone reads as the two values, which is why it's refused rather than taken as `0 ± x`. `self.sign` stays the name a style reads, so a `sign` of the author's beside it is refused rather than one quietly shadowing the other |
| 2026-11-18 | `±` and `∓` in a collection draw both branches, the + branch first, as a last variable over the two signs that a style reads as `self.sign`; anywhere else `±` is an error that names the collection to use. A collection's variable can count over a list, `for s in {-1, 1}`, in the order written. Lists in one expression zip and `for` gives every combination, but the only lists an expression holds are the signs: a range used as a value doesn't zip yet | Kris's rule, from Desmos: lists in one expression zip, and you write a `for` only when you want every combination. `±` is the list Desmos users reach for, and it always has two elements, so there's no length to mismatch. It's confined to collections because it draws two marks, and only a collection is one mark drawn many times: a `let` or a `curve` holding two values would be a list where the language has none. `for s in -1..1` counted 0 as well, silently, so the two signs needed a spelling, and the `{…}` of a choice param is the one the language already has. A list is a whole-number variable and a value picked from it, and the sign is such a list, so the IR, the oracle, strokes and SVG needed nothing new, and no golden changed but the figure converted (`016-unwrap`). Zipping ranges, Desmos's `([1...5], [1...5]²)`, waits for the consistency pass on ranges (`goals.md`), since a range means different things in different places today |
| 2026-11-18 | A point is dragged in two dimensions by one handle that sets two params, each with its own expression in the pointer: `handle z sets a = pointer.x, b = pointer.y`. Both are worked out from the same pointer before either is set, each param stops at its own range and step, and one drag holds both, so they don't hand each other back. `pointer` by itself is the pointer's point in both forms. Not chosen: a param that is itself a point | Kris's call. The figures waiting on it (`FloorCell`, `CircumcircleDemo`, `CircumNormals`, `FollowPixel`, `PowerViews`) all have a point made of two numbers, and his rule from Desmos (`migration.md`, "How a point is dragged in two dimensions") is that such a point is dragged by setting them, with an explicit inverse for anything else and none solved for. One form covers both: for `z = a + b·i` the expressions are the pointer's coordinates, and for `z = a + b·ω` on a lattice they convert the pointer to the coefficients, written out in the document, as he prefers behaviour to be. A point-valued param would need ranges that are regions and new controls, and would still need this form for the lattice. Arrows move the point on the page through the same expressions, as the one-param keys do, and since such a handle has no one range to go to the end of, Home, End and the Page keys are its long moves on the page, as in React Aria's 2D color area. To a screen reader it's a slider described as a 2D slider, whose text says both values |
| 2026-11-18 | A param with a binding has two drivers, its binding and the reader, and the reader's last action picks: setting a param hands back every other held param whose binding reads it. No new syntax. A param a handle sets gets no slider in the site's controls | Kris's principle: what a binding does is all declarable, and a page script that releases a param means the language is missing something. Bounds' page released θ by hand when q or the case changed, and the demos page didn't, so after one drag θ was stuck. Dependencies are the core's to work out, from the bindings, a choice's options, solved systems and the ranges of what a binding reads (`info.params[].reads`). A param's own range isn't its binding's value, so lowering `q` keeps a held `p` in `0..q`, as before. Holds stay in the element, since they outlive the player when a figure leaves the page, and it applies the rule in one place. This is the smallest piece of SPEC/16's rigs: the driver is selectable, picked by the reader's last touch and not by a `rig` block. Rigs proper aren't built. Time follows the same rule: setting `time` now hands back every param it drives, not only `beat`, as `play()` already did |
| 2026-11-16 | A label's mathematics is a `$…$` span in its text, holding Pencil's own notation laid out, not TeX | Kris's call, between this and TeX in the span, and he's still on the fence about TeX. One notation: what a `let` is written in is what a label is written in, and what Pencil already prints about a value (`expr`, a `polar` point's `e^(2πi/3)`) is laid out with no second printer. Grouping is by parentheses, so `{expr}` stays a value everywhere and nothing collides with it. TeX would be a second notation with backslashes, which paste detection takes to mean "this is LaTeX, convert it", and a value in a superscript would be `x^{{n}}` |
| 2026-11-16 | Symbols are written as themselves, and the editor substitutes as they're typed: `\to` → `→`, and `->` inside a label's text | Kris's call: people get text substitution in the editor, and agents can already type the symbol. So the language gets no ASCII spellings to read back, and the list is an editor's, kept in the core so it only names what the math font draws |
| 2026-11-16 | Letters written together in a span stay italic letters, and only a function's name is upright | Proposed as "multi-letter names upright", then changed while building: the square's term `2ab` is 2·a·b to a reader, and upright `ab` read as a word. A function is known by name, so `sin` and `arg` are upright and nothing else has to be guessed |
| 2026-11-16 | A fraction stacks only at the label's own size, and stays a slash inside a script or another fraction | A stacked fraction in an exponent is two more sizes down and unreadable at a label's size: `e^(2πi/3)` is what a `polar` value prints, and it has to read |
| 2026-11-16 | Terms and what a label writes about a value are laid out as mathematics under `font: math`, and are text in any other font | They are mathematics by what they are, so no mark is needed, and the role is already how a sheet says "set this as mathematics". A sheet that asks for `sans`, as the theme's tick labels do, keeps its digits in that face |
| 2026-11-16 | The core lays mathematics out, from metrics its build reads out of the math font | The command and a page have to draw one layout, and placement needs its size. The fonts are the package's now, so the core can know them: no measuring callback for mathematics, no font parser or TeX engine in the WASM, and both renderers get the same boxes by construction. Measured: the WASM went from 4.64 MB to 4.73 MB, 84 KB more (30 KB gzipped), of which about 28 KB is the table |
| 2026-11-16 | Labels are set in fonts the package ships, Libertinus (SIL Open Font License 1.1): Sans for `sans`, Serif Italic for `math`, and Libertinus Math behind both | The command read macOS's fonts, which can't be redistributed, so a figure with text failed on Linux and a render differed by machine, and `ℝ`, `∇` and `→` were boxes. Kris chose Libertinus from four sets drawn on the square and the tangents (Inter with XCharter, Inter with STIX Two, New Computer Modern, Libertinus): one family, sans included. No italic text face has the math symbols, so each role falls back to the math font |
| 2026-11-16 | A page sets labels with no kerning or ligatures | The command has no shaping, and these fonts keep their kerning in `GPOS`, which it doesn't read. Turning it off on a page makes a poster and the live figure the same width to the pixel. The cost is word spacing in a label like "arg z", until the command shapes text |
| 2026-11-16 | `--font` replaces the two text faces, and mathematics keeps the built-in math font | The core's layout is from that font's metrics, so another math font would be placed by the wrong numbers |
| 2026-11-16 | A parametric curve's stroke can be drawn per pixel, with `draw: field`, and then reads `self.dist` | The home page's hearts glow, and a glow needs a distance. Writing each heart a second time as a level set gave it one, but a level set is traced on a grid, and a heart turns back at each focus in a cusp thinner than the grid's cells, so the light stopped short of the focus by an amount that changed with the size it was drawn at. The heart as the document traces it goes through the focus exactly, and the distance is measured to that, the same way an implicit curve's is measured to its trace. It has no sign, since a traced curve has no sides, and the document says each heart once |
| 2026-11-16 | A figure keeps what a frame traced or worked out for a collection while nothing it reads changes | The hearts' grid and curves stand still while two points move, and every frame retraced and recomputed all of it: 81 ms a frame where the limaçon took 14. The key is every value the work reads, as it is for a solved system, so a frame is still a function of its inputs alone, and a test holds a figure that keeps things to what a fresh one draws |
| 2026-11-16 | A space ends a denominator: `1/2 π` is π/2, and `1/2π` is still 1/(2π) | Kris typed a fraction times π from a Desmos graph, `(k - 1)/N π`, and the π went under the bar, with a space or without. Desmos draws the bar, so there the question never comes up. Factors right against each other are one term, which is why `1/2π` reads as it does, and a space is where a reader sees the term end. Nothing in the corpus changed meaning |
| 2026-11-16 | A name that isn't a function multiplies the parentheses after it | `π(t - 1)` was an unknown function `π`. Desmos calls a name only when it is a function and multiplies otherwise, and pasted formulas rely on it. The compiler decides, since it knows what each name is, and it gives a power after the parentheses to them alone, as the product reads. A name that is nothing at all is still an unknown function, with its suggestion |
| 2026-11-16 | `pencil svg` exports what has a vector form and leaves out the rest, saying which. A style has one when its value is one color or number for a mark, for one of a collection, or along a stroke | A review found that having no vector output is what keeps people who draw in TikZ or Asymptote from trying Pencil, and Kris said to export what can be. A fill that reads `self.dist`, `blend: add` and `tone` are worked out per pixel, and drawing them flat would be a different figure, so they're left out with a line on stderr for each, and the rest of the mark is still drawn. It's written from the frame the renderers draw from, the stroke segments and the settled labels, so there's no second reading of a figure to keep in step. Claude proposed which properties export, with the corpus sorted by it, and Kris agreed |
| 2026-11-16 | An SVG draws the poster's moment at the design size, as `build` does, and labels are text | Someone exporting a figure for a paper wants the still its author chose, and `006-square` at time 0 is two axes. Text stays text so it can be selected, searched and restyled, at the cost of a viewer's font deciding the glyphs. Each label is centered on its place, so a different font moves nothing else |
| 2026-11-16 | The standard functions are built in: `sec`, `csc`, `cot`, the inverse trigonometric and hyperbolic functions, `log`, `ceil` and `round`, and they and `sin`, `cos` and `tan` take complex numbers | A formula pasted from Desmos read as `arctan(x)` and then didn't compile, because the LaTeX reader kept a name the language lacked. Reviewers playing a teacher and a hobbyist hit it first, and Kris said all the standard functions should be there. Each compiles to functions the evaluators and shaders already have, as the hyperbolic ones do, so there's one formula for the derivative, the CPU and the GPU, and nothing new to keep in step |
| 2026-11-16 | `log(x)` is base 10, `arccot(x)` is between 0 and π, and `round` sends a half away from 0 | Each is what Desmos does, and Desmos is where most pasted formulas come from: the LaTeX reader already read `\log` as base 10. `arccot` as `arctan(1/x)` would jump at 0. `ln` stays the natural logarithm |
| 2026-11-14 | The document names its poster: `poster { time: 3.5, roll: 0.6 }` | A page shows a still until a figure's first frame, and a link's preview shows nothing else. Which moment tells the most is about the figure, and the Möbius page already said it in script, as `figure.time = 3.5`. A sheet's `style figure { poster: 3.5 }` was the alternative: it lets a video's sheet and a page's differ, and can't set a param. Kris chose the document |
| 2026-11-14 | A compiled figure carries the colors its sheets name | A page takes its colors from its figure's sheets, and a compiled figure has no sheets to ask. They're in what the build writes down, too, so a page can use them before the figure runs |
| 2026-11-14 | A point can have a `z`, and a mark draws it looking down z | Kris wants the blog's 3D figures in Pencil. All three are orthographic strokes and flat fills, with no lighting, so a third coordinate that drawing drops covers them, and a figure with no `z` doesn't change. The projection is worked out when a mark is compiled, so no renderer learned anything new |
| 2026-11-14 | A point in space isn't a complex number | A point in the plane multiplies and goes through `exp`, which is why the lattice and the Möbius figures are short. None of that has a meaning in space, so it's an error there, and space gets only what a vector has |
| 2026-11-14 | The far side of a surface is a sheet's choice, read from `self.z` | The site's tube split each row into front and back paths at the nearest of 48 samples, which Kris found jumpy and hacky, and wanted as a style: "something like z < 0". The figure says where each point is, and puts the tube's axis at z = 0, so z < 0 is the side facing away however far it's rolled. The sheet fades it. The stroke is cut where the style changes, so the cut is at the crossing |
| 2026-11-14 | A stroke's `opacity` can vary along it, and overlapping parts show the most ink | A stroke had one opacity. Varying it per part, drawn into the stroke's coverage, costs no new texture or pass, and since coverage already combines by maximum, a near strand always shows over a far one it crosses, with no sorting by depth. The cost: the color can't vary along a stroke, only how much of it there is |
| 2026-11-14 | `self.z` is the figure's `z`, not the distance from the eye | With the view turned by `yaw`, distance from the eye mixes in `x`, and isn't which side of the tube a point is on. Which side faces the reader is the surface's normal against the view, and for a tube about the x axis that's the sign of `z` for any `yaw` short of a quarter turn. A figure knows its surface, so it's the one to say |
| 2026-11-14 | `use "file"` puts another file's statements in the figure, in the one set of names | Kris: the limaçon explainer's 32 beats were already a lot in one file, and helpers are written again in each figure. One namespace, because the parts of a story aren't independent: a later part drops and moves what an earlier one drew. The alternative, modules with their own names and imports, would make every part list what it takes from the others. Libraries with their own names are the next step, and build on this |
| 2026-11-14 | A used file joins once | So two parts can both `use` the helpers they need without defining them twice. Nothing has needed the same file twice, and its names would collide if it did |
| 2026-11-14 | The core says which files it needs and a host reads them | The same core runs natively and in a page, where reading is a fetch. A name is the path from the using file, so the command and a page find the same file |
| 2026-11-14 | A param's range can follow other params, and the figure keeps every param in its range | The lattice's `p` counts steps along a side of length `q`, so `p ≤ q`. The lattice's page said so in script, and the site's controls, built from what the figure says of itself, had no way to know, so `p` ran past `q`. A limit like that is part of what the param is, as its step is, so it's in the document: every host gets it, and so does a video. The core enforces it, and not only a control, because `q` can move under a `p` nobody is touching. A page asks for the range as it stands instead of being handed the expression, so it needs no evaluator. An end reads only numbers and params, which is what the lattice needs and keeps the widest range findable. Before this a binding could drive a param past its range, and only a drag was held to it. No figure did, and a test now says none does |
| 2026-11-14 | A value can read a beat written after it | Splitting the explainer by part put `let phi`, in part 2, before the beats of parts 3 to 6 that move it. Beats keep their order, so one needed early brings the beats and pauses before it. The compiled figure differs from the single file only in the order of its bindings, and its frames are the same |
| 2026-11-12 | An unknown name says what was likely meant | A name is only ever itself, so `onetwo` and a half-typed `ToCo` are errors, not the splits GeoGebra makes by what happens to be defined. The error suggests instead: a defined name the typed one starts, or is a typo or two away from, or a split into defined names, as "did you mean `one two`?". Built-ins, the figure's own functions and a sheet's names are all suggested |
| 2026-11-12 | Constants and built-ins take MathJSON's names | Kris wants expressions to conform to MathJSON, and pastes to read as they do in the Compute Engine. So `Imag`, `Conj`, `Arctanh` and `Length` became `Imaginary`, `Conjugate`, `Artanh` and `Abs`, `i` is `ImaginaryUnit`, MathJSON's constant names read as themselves, and `e^w` is `exp(w)` so Euler's identity holds exactly. `π` and the other constant glyphs stand alone, so `e^(iπ)` reads as written. The differences that stay are listed under MathJSON |
| 2026-11-12 | `e` is Euler's number, until a figure takes the name | Kris pasted an expression with Euler's `e` and got an error. GeoGebra reads `e` as Euler's until something takes the name, then rewrites the existing `e`s as `ℯ`, so naming three circles changes what a paste means. A document here has one scope, so taking the name is one explicit statement that applies to every `e` in the figure, and nothing is rewritten behind the reader's back |
| 2026-11-12 | A constant is a default a figure can take the name of | A letter is a constant in one field and a variable in another, and Desmos loses `r` and `t` once a figure assigns them. Here `let pi = …` simply wins, in the document and its sheets. The compiler writes the constant as MathJSON's `Pi`, so a figure's own `pi` is an ordinary name to the CPU and GPU paths alike, and `pi` and `π` become one symbol |
| 2026-11-12 | Factors side by side multiply, tighter than `/` | Kris wrote `2π` and it didn't parse, and pasting from Desmos brings `2(x+1)` and `a b`.
